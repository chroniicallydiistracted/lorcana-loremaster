# Constructed Deck-Building Framework

## 1. Define the deck’s job

Select Core, Infinity or the actual custom/multiplayer construction rules first. Write one sentence that states how the deck reaches its mode’s winning lore threshold (normally 20). Examples:

- Establish efficient early questers and protect tempo.
- Trade resources, stabilize, and win with high-value late characters.
- Assemble a repeatable synergy engine that converts cards or ink into lore.
- Deny opposing quests while winning through evasive or resilient threats.

Every card should advance the plan, enable the plan, protect it, or answer a matchup the plan cannot otherwise beat.

## 2. Build from role packages

Classify each card by its main role:

- Early board development
- Lore pressure
- Removal or challenge control
- Card draw or selection
- Ink acceleration
- Protection
- Recovery
- Finisher
- Synergy enabler
- Synergy payoff
- Matchup technology

A card may have several functions, but identify the role that justifies its deck slot.

## 3. Inkability

Inkable cards create flexibility; non-inkable cards usually demand a specific reason.

A practical starting range for many 60-card decks is approximately 44–48 inkable cards, but the correct number depends on:

- draw and selection;
- curve;
- ramp;
- mulligan plan;
- how many non-inkables are expensive;
- how many cards must be preserved rather than inked;
- the deck’s target ink count.

Calculate resource access under explicit keep/ink policies rather than attributing an undocumented result to probability analysis. Treat any single cutoff as a hypothesis, not a universal law. See `knowledge/strategy/probability.md`.

## 4. Curve and turn utilization

Evaluate both **cost curve** and **play curve**.

- Cost curve counts printed or alternative costs.
- Play curve asks what the deck actually wants to do each turn.

A deck with many 2-cost cards can still lack a turn-two play if those cards are reactive or conditional. Record:

- reliable turn-one plays;
- reliable turn-two plays;
- turn-three development;
- first stabilizing play;
- first card-advantage turn;
- finishers;
- alternate-cost sequences such as Shift or songs.

## 5. Copies and consistency

Use four copies when the deck wants the card early, relies on it structurally, or benefits from redundancy.

Use three when it is strong but not always needed, legendary in function, searchable, or awkward in multiples.

Use one or two when it is situational, expensive, searchable, redundant with other effects, or a narrow matchup tool.

Do not use excessive one-ofs without tutors or heavy card selection.

## 6. Interaction suite

Separate interaction by target and speed:

- small-character removal;
- large-character removal;
- damage;
- banish;
- bounce;
- exert control;
- item removal;
- location removal;
- lore loss;
- hand disruption;
- prevention/protection.

Count what each answer can actually hit in the expected meta.

## 7. Draw and card flow

Distinguish:

- true draw;
- replacement/cycling;
- top-card selection;
- tutoring;
- recursion;
- generated cards;
- conditional draw.

A deck can have many cards that say “draw” but still run out of resources if those effects are expensive, conditional, or merely replace themselves.

## 8. Synergy density

For each synergy package, count:

- enablers;
- payoffs;
- overlap cards;
- dead draws without the package;
- minimum board state;
- vulnerability to removal.

Prefer cards that function acceptably alone and become excellent together.

## 9. Goldfish and disruption tests

Goldfish at least 20 opening sequences. Record:

- missed ink;
- missed early play;
- excess uninkables;
- redundant expensive cards;
- first turn with meaningful lore pressure;
- hand size by turns 4–6.

Then test against:

- fast aggro;
- evasive pressure;
- midrange board combat;
- hard control;
- item/location engines;
- discard;
- ramp.

## 10. Revision discipline

Change a small package at a time and balance exact additions against exact cuts. Log:

- cards out;
- cards in;
- exact reason;
- expected matchup impact;
- effect on inkability and curve;
- test result.

A deck is not improved merely because its average card quality increased. The revised 60 must execute its plan more consistently against the expected field.

## 11. Ink drops and cross-format evidence

Track permanent inkwell development, temporary ink drops and singer/Shift cost
savings separately. A producer that creates drops conditionally is not guaranteed
permanent ramp. A spender's enhanced mode requires the actual payment condition.
Evaluate Set 14 changes against existing role packages and compare the exact cards
removed, curve, access to ink and matchup opportunity cost. Label prerelease builds
and cross-format extrapolations as strategic inference.
