# Matchup and Tech Guide

**Scope:** Reusable method; select the actual format, date, rules and card pool. Strategic guidance is not a measured current metagame.

## Matchup-analysis template

For each matchup, answer:

1. Who is the beatdown?
2. Which player benefits from a longer game?
3. Which opposing cards create irreversible advantage?
4. Which threats can be ignored temporarily?
5. What must the opening hand accomplish?
6. Which cards become ink more often?
7. What changes on the play versus draw?
8. What resource is the real constraint: lore, cards, board, ink, or time?
9. What is the opponent’s comeback mechanism?
10. What tech card improves the matchup without damaging the rest of the field?

## Against aggro

- Mulligan for early board and efficient ink.
- Challenge only when the trade reduces future lore more than your own quest loss.
- Avoid keeping expensive answers that cannot affect turns 1–3.
- Preserve mass interaction if the opponent is incentivized to extend.
- Know the lore threshold at which racing becomes impossible.

## Against evasive

- Count direct Evasive answers before the game.
- Establish whether you can race instead of interact.
- Avoid spending universal removal on ground threats if Evasive is the real clock.
- Prioritize effects that hit Evasive, ready characters, or multiple bodies.

## Against midrange

- Identify the pivotal challenge turns.
- Seek two-for-one value and favorable strength/willpower breakpoints.
- Do not quest into a losing board unless the lore race forces it.
- Preserve removal for value engines and finishers.

## Against control

- Present pressure in layers rather than one vulnerable wave.
- Preserve card advantage.
- Force inefficient answers.
- Know which late-game cards make continued normal play unfavorable.
- Convert safe windows into lore rather than unnecessary challenges.

## Against ramp

- Pressure the turns spent increasing ink.
- Remove or neutralize the first stabilizer.
- Do not assume card advantage matters if the opponent’s top end invalidates the board.
- Track whether ramp effects help both players.

## Against discard

- Ink expensive or redundant cards before they are stripped.
- Deploy cards rather than hoarding without purpose.
- Protect draw engines.
- Recognize when emptying your hand reduces the opponent’s discard value.
- Avoid keeping narrow cards that can be stranded.

## Against songs

- Remove or challenge high-value singers before the key song turn.
- Track singer values, not only printed costs.
- Consider whether an exerted singer creates a questing window.
- Do not walk into mass-damage songs with unnecessary board extension.

## Tech-card standard

A tech card should satisfy at least two:

- Strong against a major archetype.
- Acceptable ink when irrelevant.
- Searchable or redundant.
- Overlaps with normal deck plan.
- Improves multiple related matchups.
- Does not worsen curve or non-inkable density beyond tolerance.
