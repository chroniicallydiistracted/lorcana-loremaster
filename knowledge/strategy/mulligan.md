# Mulligan and Sequencing Guide

**Scope:** Reusable method; select the actual format, date, rules and card pool. Strategic guidance is not a measured current metagame.

## Mulligan objective

The mulligan is not a hunt for individually powerful cards. It constructs a coherent early plan while preserving enough inkable cards to execute it.

## Opening-hand questions

1. Can this hand ink on the necessary early turns?
2. Does it have a meaningful play on turns 1–3?
3. Which card is intended to be inked first?
4. Are two kept cards competing for the same role?
5. Does the matchup require a specific answer?
6. Are expensive cards supported by ramp, Shift, Singer, or draw?
7. Does the hand collapse if one early character is removed?

## Default keep rules

- Keep reliable early development.
- Keep enough inkable cards for the planned curve.
- Return expensive cards that are neither essential nor accelerated.
- Return conditional synergy cards without enablers.
- Keep narrow interaction only when the matchup makes it necessary.
- Do not keep four “good” cards that cannot be sequenced together.

## Play versus draw

On the play:

- Initiative and curve matter more.
- A proactive character can be worth more than a slow value card.
- You may need fewer reactive cards in the opening hand.

On the draw:

- You receive additional information and cards but act second.
- Early interaction and high-stat stabilizers become more valuable.
- A hand that is slightly slower may be acceptable if it trades efficiently.

## Inking sequence

Before each ink decision:

1. Plan the next two turns.
2. Identify cards required for the matchup.
3. Preserve unique answers.
4. Ink redundant legends, extra copies, or cards outside the planned curve.
5. Re-evaluate after every draw; do not follow a fixed ink script blindly.

## Sequencing principles

- Make choices and gather information before committing irreversible resources where legal.
- Play around the most likely answer, not every theoretical card.
- Resolve card selection before deciding which redundant card to ink when possible.
- Use removal when it changes the race, protects an engine, or prevents greater value.
- Quest when gaining lore is more valuable than preserving challenge access.
- Challenge when the trade prevents more opposing lore or unlocks your future quests.
- Do not exert every character automatically; ready bodies create defensive constraints.

## Songs

When sequencing a song:

- Compare the singer’s lost quest value to the ink saved.
- Protect important singers from challenge.
- Determine whether paying ink preserves a better board.
- Account for Singer and Sing Together values exactly.
- Remember that singing is a payment method; the song still resolves as an action.

## Shift

Before shifting:

- Verify exact character-name eligibility.
- Determine which damage and state carry forward.
- Account for losing access to the lower character’s printed abilities.
- Compare normal play plus another action against the Shift line.
- Anticipate removal that can turn a multi-card stack into a tempo loss.

## Post-game mulligan review

Record:

- kept and returned cards;
- intended turns 1–4;
- actual draws;
- first missed ink or unused ink;
- card that should have been returned;
- matchup-specific lesson.
