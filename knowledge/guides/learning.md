# Teaching and beginner guidance

Begin with the learner's goal and actual product: starter deck, Gateway, friendly
constructed game, prerelease, draft or cooperative Quest. Teach one usable decision
at a time and use their exact cards. Do not require competitive optimization to enjoy
the game; preserve theme, favorite characters and budget when requested.

## Suggested learning sequence

1. Explain lore and the chosen mode's victory threshold.
2. Explain inkable cards, one normal ink action, and paying with ready ink. Ink does
   not have color-specific payment; a printed ink color is a deckbuilding property.
3. Show Ready, Set, Draw and one Main phase, including drying characters.
4. Play a character, quest on an eligible later turn, and demonstrate the tradeoff
   between gaining lore and becoming challengeable.
5. Resolve a challenge with exact damage, willpower, any modifiers and resulting banishes.
6. Introduce actions, songs, items and locations only as they appear in the learner's deck.
7. Add keywords, triggered abilities, Shift, opening-hand alteration and inking choices.
8. Move to matchup priorities and advanced sequencing when basic turns are comfortable.

Separate a teaching shortcut from an exact rule. Avoid importing a stack, mana colors,
attack-the-player assumptions, instant-speed interaction or automatic healing from
other games. Use current CR when a shortcut would change a real decision.

## Practical coaching

Provide an opening-hand example and an actual sequence, with a first ink candidate
and an alternate if the draw changes. Explain why one quest or challenge helps the
lore race. Ask the learner to predict the result, then correct the specific point.
Keep feedback about controllable choices separate from an unlucky outcome.

Gateway uses its own progressive learning materials; do not skip progression rules
or invent unlock conditions. Exact contents, booklet instructions and supported
player configurations must come from that edition's official product/materials.
