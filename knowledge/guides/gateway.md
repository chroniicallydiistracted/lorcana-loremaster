# Gateway teaching and progression

**Reviewed:** 2026-10-04. **Edition:** original English Gateway materials, copyright
2024. The rules, guidebook and both Deck Tips pamphlets are now cached and indexed.
Use PDF pages when the layout or a symbol matters. These are teaching materials;
current comprehensive rules control ordinary games after the learning progression.

## Start and advance

Begin with the supplied 30-card decks. Leave the first game's decks unshuffled and
use the illustrated seven-card opening hands (Rules pp. 2–3). After that game,
shuffle and follow the Guidebook. Its goals are cooperative learning milestones
inside a competitive game: continue tracking unfinished goals across games, finish
the current game before opening a reward pack, and sort cards by deck inks.

| Unlock | Goals, paraphrased | New lesson | Guidebook page |
|---|---|---|---|
| Gate 1 | Quest with 3+ characters in one turn; make 3+ challenges in a game; quest and challenge in one turn; have Hero and Villain present together | Songs | 3–4 |
| Gate 2 | Play 2+ actions in one turn; sing 2+ songs in a game; use songs to lower opposing strength and draw a card | Items and ability costs | 5–6 |
| Gate 3 | Play 3+ items in a game; use exert-cost and banish-cost item abilities; use Support | Floodborn and Shift | 7–8 |
| Gate 4 | Shift 2+ characters in a game; gain 7+ lore in one turn; have Hero, Ally, Villain and Floodborn present; both players sing, use an item and Shift in a game | Complete decks | 9–10 |

Either player or their combined progress can satisfy goals; the last gate explicitly
requires both players' participation. Completed decks contain 66 cards apiece;
up to six cuts bring each to 60. Reward-pack membership remains a separate source
gap: the acquired Deck Tips link it by QR code, rather than listing all packs.

## Transition to current play

Rules p. 11 separates simplified teaching from ordinary play: opening-hand alteration
is optional to introduce later, and normal inking can happen during the Main phase.
Its older deck-exhaustion instruction loses on a required draw. Current CR 1.8.1.2
instead checks an empty deck at the end of its owner's turn. Explain that update
before teaching current constructed play. Guidebook p. 8's introductory Floodborn/
Shift description covers its supplied cards; it is not an assertion that every
modern Floodborn card has Shift. Likewise, mixing sets for casual learning does
not establish Core tournament eligibility for those cards today.

## Coach the decision, then the result

At each gate, ask the learner to explain one decision using the cards in front of
them. For songs, compare spending ink with using a character that could quest.
For items, identify the play cost separately from the ability cost. For Shift,
compare a dry base with a drying base and track the damage that remains.

Check understanding by changing one part of the position, then letting the learner
predict the new result. Keep a difficult rules interaction separate from completing
a progression goal. A player can win the game while still needing another game to
finish the learning goals. When deck customization begins, pair each proposed addition
with a cut and check the learner's intended format.
