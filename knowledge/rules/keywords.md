# Disney Lorcana Keywords and High-Risk Mechanics

**Project version:** 2.1
**Rules baseline:** Comprehensive Rules 2.2.0, effective 2026-07-09
**Coverage:** Every keyword in the current Comprehensive Rules, plus mechanics that commonly cause rules errors

This is a retrieval reference, not a replacement for the official rules. Reminder text on a card is abbreviated and is not the complete keyword rule. For disputed interactions, use the exact official section cited here.

Official rules: [Comprehensive Rules 2.2.0](https://files.disneylorcana.com/Comprehensive-Rules_2.2.0-EN.pdf)

---

# 1. Keyword system

The CR 2.2.0 keyword list is: Alert, Bodyguard, Boost, Challenger, Evasive, Reckless, Resist, Rush, Shift, Singer, Sing Together, Support, Vanish, and Ward. (CR 8.1.)

A keyword is shorthand for a defined ability. Multiple instances behave according to the rules for that keyword:

- A redundant non-numerical keyword generally does not create extra function merely because it appears twice.
- Numerical keywords such as Resist +N and Challenger +N can combine when several effects grant them. (CR 8.1.)

---

# 2. Alert

**Rule:** CR 8.2.

Alert is a static ability that lets the character ignore the challenge restriction created by Evasive. A character with Alert can legally challenge an Evasive character even if the challenger does not itself have Evasive. (CR 8.2.1.)

Alert does **not** grant Evasive. The Alert character can still be challenged normally unless another effect gives it Evasive or otherwise protects it. (CR 8.2.2.)

### Common mistakes

- Alert does not allow a character to challenge while drying.
- Alert does not bypass Bodyguard, Ward, ready-character restrictions, or unrelated challenge limiters.
- Alert is relevant when selecting the challenge target; it is not a damage modifier.

---

# 3. Bodyguard

**Rule:** CR 8.3.

Bodyguard represents two static abilities:

1. The character may enter play exerted instead of ready. (CR 8.3.2.)
2. When an opponent chooses one of your characters to challenge, they must choose an eligible Bodyguard character if able. If several eligible Bodyguards exist, the opponent can choose among them. (CR 8.3.3.)

### Common mistakes

- Entering exerted is optional unless another effect requires it.
- A ready Bodyguard normally does not force a challenge because characters normally must be exerted to be challenged.
- An ineligible Bodyguard does not block all challenges. Evasive and other genuine challenge limiters can affect whether it is an eligible target. Ward does not prevent a character from being challenged and therefore does not make a Bodyguard ineligible for that reason.
- Bodyguard protects characters, not locations, unless an effect says otherwise.

---

# 4. Boost

**Rule:** CR 8.4.

Boost N is a sequential effect usable once during the controller’s turn at a time they could use an activated ability. The player may pay **N ink** to put **one card** from the top of their deck facedown under the card with Boost. (CR 8.4.1.)

The number N is the ink payment. It is **not** the number of cards placed underneath.

No player may look at the front of a card placed facedown underneath another card with Boost, including its controller. (CR 8.4.2.)

A card underneath another card:

- Is not independently in play.
- Was not played merely because Boost placed it there.
- Does not normally contribute its printed characteristics unless an effect counts cards underneath. (CR 8.4.3.)

### Common mistakes

- Boost is not automatically an activated ability simply because it is used at activated-ability timing; use its official sequential-effect definition.
- Each instance is limited to once during the controller’s turn as defined by the keyword.
- Putting a character underneath does not trigger “when you play a character.”
- Do not reveal or identify the card unless another effect specifically permits it.

---

# 5. Challenger +N

**Rule:** CR 8.5.

While the character is the challenger in a challenge, Challenger +N increases its Strength by N. Numerical Challenger effects combine. (CR 8.5.1.)

The bonus does not apply when that character is being challenged. (CR 8.5.2.)

### Common mistakes

- Challenger does not give permission to challenge.
- Challenger does not increase Strength while questing, singing, or outside a challenge.
- The bonus applies while challenging a location even though the location normally deals no damage back.

---

# 6. Evasive

**Rule:** CR 8.6.

Evasive is a static challenge limiter. A character or other type with Evasive normally cannot be challenged except by a character with Evasive. (CR 8.6.1.)

Alert separately permits its character to ignore this Evasive limiter. Effects can also directly permit or prohibit a challenge.

### Common mistakes

- Evasive does not prevent actions or abilities from choosing the card; Ward addresses choosing.
- Evasive does not stop global effects.
- A character can have Evasive only during a stated duration or condition. Check whether the ability is currently active.
- An Evasive location follows the type-specific wording of the effect or keyword.

---

# 7. Reckless

**Rule:** CR 8.7.

Reckless represents two static abilities:

1. The character cannot quest. (CR 8.7.2.)
2. The controller cannot declare the end of their turn while that character is ready and able to challenge an opposing character or location. (CR 8.7.3.)

A Reckless character can still be exerted to sing a song or pay an ability cost if legal. If it is no longer ready, or no legal challenge exists, the requirement no longer prevents ending the turn. (CR 8.7.4.)

### Common mistakes

- Reckless does not say the character’s first action must be a challenge.
- The controller may use another legal way to exert the character.
- The controller chooses among legal challenges; the opponent does not choose merely because the character is Reckless.
- A Reckless character that cannot challenge because of Evasive or another limiter does not create an impossible requirement.

---

# 8. Resist +N

**Rule:** CR 8.8.

Resist +N reduces damage that would be **dealt** to the character or location by N. Numerical Resist effects combine. (CR 8.8.1.)

If the reduction brings the damage to zero, the card takes no damage counters from that event. (CR 8.8.2.)

Damage **put** or **moved** onto the card is not reduced by Resist. (CR 8.8.3.)

### Common mistakes

- Resist does not increase willpower.
- Resist does not remove damage already marked.
- An effect stating that damage cannot be reduced overrides Resist.
- If damage is reduced to zero, the source is still considered to have dealt damage, but the target takes no damage and receives no damage counters. Damage-dealt triggers can still occur. (CR 1.9.5.)

---

# 9. Rush

**Rule:** CR 8.9.

Rush permits the character to challenge as though it had been in play at the beginning of its controller’s turn. (CR 8.9.1.)

Rush bypasses drying only for challenging. It does not allow the character to:

- Quest while drying.
- Sing while drying.
- Pay another exert cost while drying.
- Ignore challenge limiters such as Evasive or Bodyguard.

---

# 10. Shift and Shift variants

**Rule:** CR 8.10.

Shift is an alternate cost to play a character. It is still a play event and can cause on-play triggers. The top character is placed on the eligible card or cards specified by the applicable Shift ability. (CR 8.10.1.)

## 10.1 Standard Shift

Standard Shift permits the character to be played for its Shift cost on top of another character in play with the same character name. (CR 8.10.1.)

## 10.2 State inherited by the shifted character

- If shifted onto an exerted card, it enters exerted. (CR 8.10.2.)
- An effect requiring the new character to enter exerted still applies even if the lower card was ready. (CR 8.10.3.)
- If shifted onto a dry character, it is dry; if shifted onto a drying character, it is drying. (CR 8.10.4.)
- If shifted onto an eligible non-character, it is dry only if that lower card has been in play since the beginning of the controller’s turn; otherwise it is drying. (CR 8.10.4.1.)
- When shifting onto two cards, any drying lower character makes the top character drying, and any exerted lower card makes it exerted. (CR 8.10.4.2.)
- The shifted character retains the damage marked on the card or stack beneath it. (CR 8.10.6.)

The top card does not gain the printed abilities of the cards beneath it. Effects already applying to the lower character can continue to apply as the rules define. (CR 8.10.5.)

When the shifted character leaves play, all cards in its stack move to the same destination zone and cease to be a stack. (CR 8.10.7.)

## 10.3 Classification Shift

A `[Classification] Shift` ability permits the card to shift onto another character with the stated classification rather than requiring a matching character name. (CR 8.10.8.1.)

## 10.4 Universal Shift

Universal Shift permits the card to shift onto another character in play, regardless of name or classification, subject to the ability’s other requirements. (CR 8.10.8.2.)

## 10.5 Duo Shift

Duo Shift applies to a dual-named character. The player must have two characters that each match one of the two names. The Duo Shift card is placed on top of both lower characters, which are stacked beneath it in either order. (CR 8.10.8.3.)

## 10.6 Combo Shift

Combo Shift provides two Shift routes within the ability:

- Shift onto one character sharing one of the card’s names; or
- Shift onto two characters that each share one of the card’s names.

When using two cards, stack them underneath in either order. (CR 8.10.8.4.)

## 10.7 Temporary Shift

Temporary Shift functions as Shift but creates a delayed end-of-turn ability. If the top card is still in play at the end of the turn, remove its damage and return **only the top card** to its owner’s hand. The underlying card or stack remains and is handled under the current stack rules. (CR 8.10.8.5.)

## 10.8 Potato Shift

Potato Shift permits the character to shift onto an item named `Potato`. The lower item’s time in play determines whether the shifted character is dry or drying. (CR 8.10.8.6.)

## 10.9 Combined Shift variants

If an ability combines two or more Shift variants, all conditions of those combined variants must be met. For example, Temporary Red Panda Shift requires an eligible Red Panda and also creates Temporary Shift’s delayed return. (CR 8.10.9.)

### Common Shift mistakes

- A character played for free cannot separately choose to pay a Shift alternate cost. (CR 4.3.6.)
- Shift does not erase damage from the lower card.
- The top card is played; the lower card is not replayed.
- A lower card’s printed activated ability is not inherited.
- Copy limits and name matching use current official identity rules, especially for dual names.
- Removing or returning the top card moves the stack as required unless Temporary Shift explicitly returns only the top card.

# 11. Singer N

**Rule:** CR 8.11.

Singer N changes the value the character counts as when paying the singing alternate cost. The character can exert to sing as though its cost were N instead of its normal ink cost. (CR 8.11.1.)

Singer changes the character’s value only for singing during the play process; it does not change printed cost for unrelated effects. (CR 8.11.2.)

An effect that says the character counts as having +N cost to sing combines with Singer’s value. (CR 8.11.3.)

### Common mistakes

- Singer does not let a drying character sing.
- Singer does not reduce the song’s printed cost for other effects.
- Singing exerts the character as an alternate cost; it is not an activated ability of the character.

---

# 12. Sing Together N

**Rule:** CR 8.12.

Sing Together permits **one or more** ready characters controlled by the player and, where applicable, teammates to be exerted as an alternate cost. Their combined ink costs must meet or exceed N. (CR 8.12.1-8.12.2.)

Zero characters cannot satisfy Sing Together even if another effect would appear to reduce a payment.

Every chosen character that sings satisfies a trigger condition such as “whenever this character sings a song.” (CR 8.12.3.)

### Common mistakes

- Each singer must be ready and legally able to exert.
- Drying characters cannot sing unless an effect specifically permits it.
- Singer values and effects modifying singing cost must be applied under their exact wording.
- Sing Together does not require every singer to meet N individually; use the combined eligible value.

---

# 13. Support

**Rule:** CR 8.13.

Support is a triggered ability. When the character quests, its controller may add that character’s current Strength to another chosen character’s Strength for the turn. (CR 8.13.1.)

### Common mistakes

- Support triggers from questing; it is not part of the lore-gain amount.
- The Support character cannot choose itself as “another” character.
- The bonus uses the Support character’s Strength as determined for the effect.
- The bonus lasts for the stated turn and can affect challenge damage later that turn.
- Ward can prevent the opposing player from choosing a card, but a player can normally choose their own Ward character for their own effect because Ward restricts opponents.

---

# 14. Vanish

**Rule:** CR 8.14.

Vanish is a triggered ability. It triggers when the character is chosen by an opponent as part of resolving an **action’s effect**. The triggered ability banishes that character. (CR 8.14.1.)

The action completes resolving before the Vanish trigger resolves. If the character has already moved to another zone, the Vanish trigger resolves without banishing it. (CR 8.14.2.)

### Common mistakes

Vanish does not automatically trigger from:

- An opposing character’s ability.
- An opposing item or location ability.
- A challenge.
- An effect that affects all or each without choosing.
- The controller’s own action.

Always confirm that the opponent is resolving an **action** and that the action’s effect chooses the character.

---

# 15. Ward

**Rule:** CR 8.15.

Ward prevents opponents from choosing the card while resolving an effect. (CR 8.15.1.)

Effects that do not choose still affect it. (CR 8.15.2.)

### Ward does not prevent

- A legal challenge.
- Effects affecting each or all applicable cards.
- Game-state checks.
- The card’s controller choosing it with their own effect.
- A replacement or static effect that applies without choosing.

### Common mistake

Do not infer choosing merely because a player can identify which objects an area effect affects. Look for the rules meaning of “chosen” in the effect.

---

# 16. Drying

**Rules:** CR 5.1, CR 3.2.2, and applicable turn actions.

A character is normally drying until the Set step of its controller’s next turn. While drying, it cannot:

- Quest.
- Challenge, except through Rush or another specific permission.
- Sing.
- Pay an exert cost.

Drying does not turn off static abilities, triggers, or abilities without an exert cost.

Shift can cause the top character to be dry or drying based on the lower card or cards. Use CR 8.10 rather than assuming every shifted character can act immediately.

---

# 17. Singing songs

**Rules:** CR 5.4.4, CR 8.11, CR 8.12.

A song is an action. It can normally be played by paying ink or by exerting an eligible ready character whose cost is at least the song’s cost. Singer and Sing Together provide other singing values or multiple singers.

Singing is an alternate cost. The song is still played and resolves as an action, then goes to the discard.

Important distinctions:

- A character must be legally able to exert.
- Singing sacrifices that character’s ready state and therefore its immediate quest/challenge options.
- A sung song can trigger “when you play an action/song” and “when this character sings” abilities as applicable.

---

# 18. Inking

**Game rule:** CR 4.2.
**Tournament overlay:** TR 5.4-5.5.

Normally, the active player can take the ink action once during their Main phase by selecting an inkable card from hand and putting it into the inkwell.

In sanctioned tournaments:

1. Announce the full name.
2. Allow verification of the inkwell symbol.
3. Leave it faceup for the turn.
4. Turn it facedown at end of turn.

A card placed into the inkwell by another effect is not automatically the normal once-per-turn ink action. Follow that effect for face orientation and ready/exerted state.

---

# 19. Playing versus putting or moving

**Rules:** CR 4.3, CR 6.7, CR 7.

A card is played only through the play process. These are not automatically play events:

- Put into play.
- Put into the inkwell.
- Return to hand.
- Move to a location.
- Put under a card.
- Reveal.
- Shifted-under cards moving with a stack.

This distinction controls on-play triggers and effects that care about a card being played.

# 20. Playing for free

**Rule:** CR 4.3.6.

A free play ignores the costs of playing the card but still follows the play process. The player cannot choose another alternate cost such as Shift. Legal choices, prohibitions, timing, and non-cost instructions remain applicable.

A modifier that applies to the next play can still be consumed or considered under current rule 4.3.6 even when no ink is ultimately paid. Check the exact modifier rather than assuming “free” makes every cost interaction irrelevant.

---

# 21. Choosing, each, all, and up to

**Rules:** CR 6.2, CR 6.7, CR 8.15.

- “Chosen” requires a legal choice.
- “Each” and “all” affect the described group without individually choosing every object.
- “Up to N” permits choosing fewer than N, including zero, unless another requirement applies.
- An instruction for exactly N requires as many legal choices as possible under the resolution rules.

Ward and Vanish depend heavily on these distinctions.

---

# 22. Damage dealt, put, moved, and removed

**Rules:** CR 1.9 and CR 8.8.

- **Dealt:** a damage event that can be reduced or prevented.
- **Put:** place damage counters without necessarily dealing damage.
- **Moved:** transfer marked damage as instructed.
- **Removed:** take marked damage counters off a card.

Resist applies only to damage dealt. A trigger requiring damage dealt does not automatically trigger from damage put or moved.

---

# 23. Ready during a challenge

**Rule:** CR 4.6; clarified in CR 2.2.0.

Readying a participant during an ongoing challenge does not automatically cancel, rewind, or create a separate challenge. Continue the official challenge procedure and apply current rules for whether the character remains a participant and how subsequent steps resolve.

Never answer this interaction from visual state alone; identify the exact point in the challenge when the readying effect resolves.

---

# 24. Triggered abilities and the bag

**Rules:** CR 6.2 and CR 7.7.

Whenever a triggered ability’s condition is met, that ability is added to the bag by the player whose card generated it. Simultaneous triggers are added simultaneously. If the condition is met while another effect is resolving, the ability is added immediately but waits to resolve until the current effect is completely resolved. (CR 7.7.3.)

The bag is not a freely reordered stack, and players do not pre-order all their waiting abilities. At a bag check, the active player chooses and fully resolves one of their triggered effects, then continues resolving their effects one at a time until none remain. The bag then passes to the next player in turn order. Perform the required game-state check after each resolved effect, and add any new triggered abilities when their conditions are met. (CR 7.7.4-7.7.6.)

---

# 25. “Once during your turn” and new objects

A usage restriction is tracked for the specific ability instance or object defined by the rules. When a card changes zones, it normally becomes a new object. Do not automatically assume a once-per-turn ability remains used—or resets—without applying the exact zone-change and ability rules.

Boost specifically contains its own once-during-your-turn limitation. Other abilities can use different wording and tracking.

---

# 26. Locations

**Rules:** CR 3.2.2, CR 4.6-4.7, CR 5.6.

- Location lore is gained during the controller’s Set step.
- Locations can be challenged regardless of being “ready,” because the usual exerted-character target rule is not the same for locations.
- Locations normally do not deal challenge damage.
- A character can be at only one location.
- Moving a character requires the location’s move cost unless an effect provides another method.

# 28. Ink drops and new mechanics

Ink drops are a Set 14 resource mechanic. Do not add them to the CR 2.2.0 keyword
list or invent a CR keyword number. See `knowledge/rules/ink-drops.md` for supported
introductory behavior and the source-verification boundary.
