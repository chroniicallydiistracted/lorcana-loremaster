# Disney Lorcana Comprehensive Rules - Near-Comprehensive Operational Digest

**Project version:** 2.1
**Rules baseline:** Disney Lorcana Comprehensive Rules 2.2.0
**Official effective date:** 2026-07-09
**Regional scope:** Rules are globally applicable; verify the relevant region for releases and events
**Scope:** All ordinary Lorcana gameplay; product/mode overrides require their own instructions

This document is a retrieval-oriented digest of the current official rules. It is designed to make ordinary gameplay and interaction questions easier to analyze, but it does **not** replace the official Comprehensive Rules. For a disputed interaction, tournament-impacting ruling, or exact wording question, consult the official PDF and applicable release notes.

Official rules: [Comprehensive Rules 2.2.0](https://files.disneylorcana.com/Comprehensive-Rules_2.2.0-EN.pdf)

---

## 1. Authority, terminology, and interpretation

### 1.1 Official language and card text

The current English card text and current English rules are the official gameplay references. Translations, printed reminder text, promotional layouts, and older printings can differ from current functional text. Reminder text is explanatory and is not itself rules text. (CR 1.1.4-1.1.7.)

When a card image, catalog row, older printing, translation, or strategy guide conflicts with a current official correction, use the current official English functional text.

### 1.2 Golden rules

Apply these principles before ordinary rules:

1. A specific card effect can override a general rule when the two conflict. (CR 1.2.1.)
2. A prohibition or limiter takes priority over an instruction that permits or requires the prohibited event. In practical terms, “can’t” defeats “can.” (CR 1.2.2.)
3. If part of an effect cannot be performed, perform as much as possible unless the effect establishes a cost, condition, or dependency that requires otherwise. (CR 1.2.3 and CR 6.2.)
4. Cards and effects affect only the objects and players described by their text. Do not extend an effect by analogy. (CR 1.2.)

### 1.3 Names, versions, and full names

A character’s **name** and **version** are separate card parts. The **full name** combines both, such as `Stitch - Rock Star`. Rules and deck construction can refer to the name, version, or full name differently. (CR 5.2 and CR 5.3.)

A character with multiple names has each name and the combined name defined by CR 5.3.1. Apply current rules and Set 13 release notes when an effect asks whether names match, especially for Shift, Duo Shift, and copy limits. Do not resolve name identity from punctuation or a localized title alone.

### 1.4 Classifications

Classifications appear on the classification line and can be referenced by effects. A card can have multiple classifications. The classification list in the CR 2.2.0 baseline is: Alien, Ally, Boss, Broom, Captain, Colossus, Deity, Detective, Dinosaur, Dragon, Dreamborn, Entangled, Fairy, Floodborn, Gargoyle, Ghost, Giant, Hero, Hunny, Hyena, Illusion, Inventor, King, Knight, Madrigal, Mentor, Monster, Musketeer, Pirate, Prince, Princess, Puppy, Queen, Racer, Red Panda, Robot, Seven Dwarfs, Sorcerer, Storyborn, Super, Team, Tigger, Titan, Toy, Villain, Vineling, and Whisper. Use the current official classification layer when older and newer printings differ. (CR 5.3.3.2.)

## 2. Core game concepts

### 2.1 Players and ownership

Each card has an owner, normally the player who began the game with it in their deck or Limited pool. A card’s controller can change through effects, but ownership does not. When a card changes zones, it normally goes to the corresponding zone of its owner unless an effect or rule says otherwise. (CR 1.3 and CR 7.)

### 2.2 Lore and winning

A normal player wins by reaching at least 20 lore, subject to simultaneous game-state checks and special format rules. Lore can be gained or lost, but it cannot normally be reduced below zero. (CR 1.11 and CR 2.1.)

If multiple players meet a win or loss condition at the same time, apply the current game-state-check and multiplayer rules rather than assuming the active player automatically wins. (CR 1.8 and CR 9.)

### 2.3 Drawing cards and an empty deck

To draw, move the top card of the deck to the hand without revealing it unless instructed. If a player attempts to draw while their deck has no cards, no card is drawn; that failed draw does not itself cause an immediate loss. A player loses if their turn ends while their deck contains no cards. This allows the remainder of the turn to proceed and permits the deck to be replenished before the turn-end game-state check. (CR 1.8.1.2 and CR 2.3.3.2.)

### 2.4 Public and hidden information

The identity and order of cards in a deck are hidden. A hand is hidden from opponents. The discard is public. Cards in the inkwell are generally hidden once facedown, subject to tournament procedure and effects. Cards revealed by an effect remain revealed only for the duration required by that effect. (CR 7.)

### 2.5 Randomization

Whenever a deck is shuffled, its order must be randomized. In tournament play, additional presentation and opponent-shuffle procedures apply. (CR 2.2; Tournament Rules.)

## 3. Costs, permissions, and restrictions

### 3.1 Costs

A cost is something that must be paid to play a card, activate an ability, or perform an instructed action. Costs can include ink, exerting, discarding, banishing, returning a card, or another specified event. A player cannot choose to pay a cost they cannot fully pay. (CR 1.5.)

A cost is distinct from an effect. Paying a cost is not the same as resolving an instruction that happens to move, discard, or banish a card.

### 3.2 Ink costs and payment

To pay ink, exert the required number of ready cards in the inkwell. Unless an effect says otherwise, ink cards do not have individual denominations; each ready ink card pays one ink. (CR 1.5 and CR 4.3.)

### 3.3 Alternate costs

An alternate cost replaces the normal cost of playing a card. Examples include:

- Singing a song
- Shift and its variants
- An effect that explicitly provides another way to play a card

A player cannot combine two alternate costs for the same play unless an effect explicitly allows it. (CR 1.5.5, CR 4.3, CR 5.4.4, CR 8.10-8.12.)

### 3.4 Additional costs

An additional cost is normally paid in addition to the selected normal or alternate cost. However, when an ability or effect instructs a player to play a card for free, that player ignores **all costs of playing that card**, including any additional play costs, and cannot choose another alternate cost. Distinguish a cost of playing the card from a choice, limiter, prohibition, or non-cost instruction that still applies during the play process or resolution. (CR 1.5 and CR 6.1.7.)

### 3.5 Cost increases, reductions, and minimums

Determine the applicable cost, then apply increases and reductions in the order required by the rules. A modifier that changes how a player pays does not necessarily change the printed ink cost for effects that inspect that number. (CR 4.3.4-4.3.6.)

### 3.6 Playing a card for free

When an ability or effect instructs a player to play a card for free, the player follows the normal play process but ignores all costs of playing that card. The player cannot choose another alternate cost, such as Shift, for that play. Legal choices, limiters, prohibitions, and instructions that are not costs still apply. (CR 4.3.2.2 and CR 6.1.7.)

A payment modifier that applies to the “next” card or action can still be used up by a free play when the current rules say that modifier applies, even though no ink is ultimately paid. Do not assume every reduction is irrelevant merely because the card is free. (CR 4.3.6.)

### 3.7 Illegal actions

A player cannot knowingly begin an action that is illegal under known information. If a proposed action is discovered to be illegal during its process, reverse it to the extent required by the rules or call a judge in tournament play. Do not use an illegal attempt to gain information. (CR 1.7; Tournament Rules and Play Correction Guidelines.)

## 4. Game-state checks

Game-state checks are automatic rules checks performed at specified times, including after actions and effects resolve and before triggered abilities continue resolving from the bag. They are not optional and do not use the bag. (CR 1.8.)

The current automatic game-state checks include:

- A player with at least 20 lore wins.
- A player whose turn ends with no cards in their deck loses.
- In multiplayer, a player wins when they are the last remaining player.
- A character with damage equal to or greater than its current willpower is banished.
- A location with damage equal to or greater than its current willpower is banished.

Illegal actions and tournament state repairs are handled under the illegal-action and correction procedures; they are not an invented catch-all game-state check. If several game-state consequences apply together, process them using the official simultaneous-check procedure. Then identify triggered abilities caused by those events and add them to the bag in the proper order. (CR 1.7, CR 1.8, and CR 6.2.)

## 5. Damage

### 5.1 Damage dealt

Damage that is **dealt** is an event that can be modified, prevented, or reduced. Challenge damage is normally dealt simultaneously by the challenging and challenged characters. Locations do not normally deal challenge damage unless an effect gives them a way to do so. (CR 1.9 and CR 4.6.)

### 5.2 Damage counters

After damage is dealt, the corresponding number of damage counters is placed on the affected character or location. Marked damage remains until removed or the card leaves the relevant zone, unless an effect says otherwise. (CR 1.9.)

### 5.3 Putting or moving damage

An effect that **puts** or **moves** damage is not necessarily dealing damage. Damage reduction such as Resist applies to damage that would be dealt, not merely to counters put or moved. (CR 1.9 and CR 8.8.3.)

### 5.4 Damage calculation

When damage would be dealt:

1. Start with the base amount.
2. Apply applicable modifiers and replacement effects.
3. Apply prevention or reduction, including Resist, unless the damage cannot be reduced.
4. Deal the resulting amount.
5. Place damage counters.
6. Perform game-state checks.

If damage is reduced to zero, the source is still considered to have dealt damage, but the affected character or location takes no damage and receives no damage counters from that event. Effects that trigger when damage is dealt can therefore still trigger even when the final amount is zero. (CR 1.9.5.)

### 5.5 Willpower modifiers

Changing willpower does not deal or remove damage. If marked damage becomes equal to or greater than current willpower after a modifier, the card is banished during the next game-state check. (CR 1.8-1.9.)

## 6. Decks and game materials

### 6.1 Constructed decks

The Comprehensive Rules provide general deck concepts; sanctioned Core construction is governed by the Tournament Rules. For date-aware Core/Infinity scope, see `knowledge/formats/legality.md`. Ordinary Constructed has a minimum 60-card deck, no more than two ink types unless a card modifies construction, and no more than four copies of the same full English card name. Verify event date, bans, and modifiers. (CR 1.10; Tournament Rules 1.3.1 and 1.6.)

### 6.2 Deck-construction modifying cards

Current rules allow cards to modify deck-construction requirements. Apply the modifier only as written and confirm that the card itself is legal for the format. Do not infer broader permission, such as unlimited inks or copies, unless the effect explicitly grants it. (CR 1.10 and CR 2.2.0 updates.)

### 6.3 Limited decks

Sealed and Draft use only the player’s supplied or drafted pool. Tournament Rules set their deck sizes and remove ordinary Constructed ink and copy restrictions. See `knowledge/rules/tournament.md` and `knowledge/formats/limited.md`.

### 6.4 Tracking information

Players need reliable ways to track lore, damage, and other game information. Use clear counters or markers that cannot be confused with cards or hidden information. Tournament Rules can impose additional requirements. (CR 1.10; Tournament Rules.)

## 7. Setup and mulligan

### 7.1 Pre-game setup

For a normal game: (CR 2.2.)

1. Each player presents and randomizes their legal deck.
2. Determine the starting player by the applicable casual or tournament method.
3. Each player draws seven cards.
4. Beginning with the starting player, each player may choose any number of cards from their opening hand to mulligan.
5. Put the selected cards on the bottom of the deck.
6. Draw until the hand again contains seven cards.
7. After all players have completed this choice, each player who changed their hand shuffles their deck.
8. Complete any remaining setup instructions and begin the first turn.

The returned cards are not shuffled before replacements are drawn. This prevents a player from immediately redrawing a returned card during the replacement draw. (CR 2.2.2.)

### 7.2 First turn draw

The starting player skips the entire Draw step of the first turn in a normal two-player game unless a format-specific rule says otherwise. Because the step is skipped, no part of that step occurs, including effects that would occur specifically during that Draw step. (CR 3.2.3.1 and the rules for skipping steps.)

### 7.3 Pre-game effects

If an effect modifies setup, opening hand size, first-player procedure, or deck construction, apply it at the specific point defined by the current rules and effect. Do not treat it as a normal in-game trigger unless the effect says so. (CR 2.)

## 8. Turn structure

A turn has three phases: Start-of-Turn, Main, and End-of-Turn. (CR 3.1.)

## 8.1 Start-of-Turn phase: Ready, Set, Draw

The Start-of-Turn phase contains three named steps. (CR 3.2.)

### Ready step

The active player readies all of their cards in play and in their inkwell unless an effect prevents a particular card from readying. This step is a game procedure rather than a player-selected action. (CR 3.2.1.)

### Set step

During Set: (CR 3.2.2.)

1. All of the active player’s characters that are in play stop drying during the Set step. (CR 3.2.2.1.)
2. The active player gains lore from their locations according to each location’s current lore value.
3. Start-of-turn triggered abilities are identified and processed using the bag.

Location lore is gained during Set, not during Ready or Draw.

### Draw step

The active player draws one card. In a normal two-player game, the starting player skips the entire Draw step on their first turn. Then relevant triggers are processed as required. (CR 3.2.3 and CR 3.2.3.1.)

## 8.2 Main phase

During the Main phase, the active player can perform legal turn actions in any sequence and can repeat actions when permitted. There is no fixed ordering such as “ink before playing” unless an effect imposes one. (CR 3.3 and CR 4.1.)

The normal turn actions are:

- Ink a card
- Play a card
- Use an activated ability
- Quest
- Challenge
- Move a character to a location

The player may pass between actions while resolving triggered abilities and game-state checks as required.

## 8.3 End-of-Turn phase

During End-of-Turn: (CR 3.4.)

- End-of-turn triggered abilities occur and resolve using the bag.
- Effects lasting “this turn” or “until end of turn” expire at the rule-defined point.
- Damage is **not** automatically removed.
- Tournament inkwell procedure requires cards inked that turn to be turned facedown at the end of the turn if they were left faceup for verification.
- The turn passes to the next player after required checks and cleanup.

Do not confuse “until the start of your next turn” with “this turn”; they expire at different points.

## 9. Turn actions

## 9.1 Ink a card

Normally, a player may ink one card per turn during their Main phase. (CR 4.2.)

General procedure:

1. Choose a card in hand with the inkwell symbol unless an effect permits another card.
2. Reveal enough information to verify that it can be inked.
3. Put it into the inkwell as instructed by the rules.
4. It becomes a facedown ink card and can be used to pay ink when ready.

In sanctioned tournaments, a newly inked card remains faceup through the turn and is turned facedown at end of turn so the opponent can verify the card. That is a tournament procedure layered onto the game rule. (Tournament Rules 3.4.)

A card put into the inkwell by an effect is not necessarily the player’s normal once-per-turn ink action. Follow the effect’s exact instructions for ready/exerted status and visibility.

## 9.2 Play a card

The play process includes declaring the card, establishing legality, making required choices at the correct time, determining the applicable cost, paying it, and moving/resolving the card as appropriate. (CR 4.3 and CR 6.7.)

- A character, item, or location enters the Play zone.
- An action resolves its effect and then goes to the discard.
- A song is an action subtype and follows action resolution.
- A character played with Shift is placed on the eligible card or cards as the Shift rules require.

A card is “played” only when an instruction uses the play process. A card merely put, moved, returned, or revealed is not automatically played. (CR 4.3 and CR 6.7.)

## 9.3 Use an activated ability

An activated ability has a stated cost and effect. To use it: (CR 4.4 and CR 6.3.)

1. Confirm the source is in a zone where the ability functions.
2. Confirm timing and any once-per-turn or other restrictions.
3. Make required choices at the rule-defined time.
4. Pay the complete cost.
5. Add or resolve the effect according to the activation rules.

A drying character cannot pay an exert cost unless a rule or effect permits it, but abilities that do not require exertion can function normally. (CR 5.1 and CR 6.)

## 9.4 Quest

To quest with a character: (CR 4.5.)

1. Choose a ready character you control.
2. Confirm it is not drying and no limiter prevents it from questing, such as Reckless.
3. Exert it as the quest action.
4. Gain lore equal to its current lore value.
5. Identify quest triggers, perform game-state checks, and process the bag.

Questing is not an effect that chooses the questing character; it is a turn action.

## 9.5 Challenge

To challenge: (CR 4.6.)

1. Choose a ready character you control as the challenger.
2. Confirm that the character was in play at the beginning of your Set step, or that an effect such as Rush permits it to challenge as though it had been. Then check all other requirements and limiters. A character that entered play after the beginning of the Set step is not eligible merely because it later becomes ready or stops being described as drying.
3. Choose a legal opposing character or location. An opposing character normally must be exerted unless an effect permits ready characters to be challenged. A location can be challenged while ready because locations do not use ready/exert status in the same way.
4. Apply requirements and limiters such as Bodyguard, Evasive, Alert, and card effects.
5. Exert the challenger and establish the challenge.
6. Process challenge triggers at their required steps.
7. Characters deal challenge damage simultaneously based on current Strength, unless an effect changes that event. A challenged location receives damage from the challenger but does not normally deal damage back.
8. Perform game-state checks and process resulting triggers.

### A character readied during a challenge

Rules 2.2.0 explicitly define what happens when a character is readied during an ongoing challenge. Do not assume that readying automatically cancels, rewinds, or creates a new challenge. Continue using the current challenge steps and rules for whether the character remains a participant. (CR 4.6 and CR 2.2.0 changes.)

### Leaving the challenge

If a participant leaves the relevant zone, ceases to be the same object, or an effect otherwise changes its participation, apply the exact challenge-step rules. Damage and triggers already created are not automatically reversed.

## 9.6 Move a character to a location

To move a character: (CR 4.7.)

1. Choose a character you control and a location you control that can receive it.
2. Pay the location’s move cost, subject to modifiers.
3. Move the character to that location.

A character can be at only one location at a time. Moving is not playing the character and does not make it a new object. Effects can move a character without using the normal turn action or move cost when stated.

## 10. Card states and object identity

## 10.1 Ready and exerted

A ready card is upright and can be used for actions or costs that require it. An exerted card is turned sideways and cannot be exerted again until readied. Readying does not remove damage or make an object new. (CR 5.1.)

## 10.2 Drying

A character is normally drying from the time it enters play until the Set step of its controller’s next turn. While drying, it cannot quest, challenge, or pay an exert cost unless an effect specifically permits that action, such as Rush allowing a challenge. (CR 5.1 and CR 8.9.)

Drying does not disable passive abilities, triggered abilities, or activated abilities without an exert cost.

## 10.3 Faceup and facedown

A facedown card normally has only the characteristics assigned by the rule or effect that placed it facedown. An ink card in the inkwell does not retain its visible card characteristics for ordinary gameplay. A facedown card under another card follows the under-card rules. (CR 5.1 and CR 7.)

## 10.4 New objects after zone changes

When a card changes zones, it normally becomes a new object with no memory of its previous existence, unless a rule or effect explicitly tracks it. This principle determines whether once-per-turn use, damage, choices, and delayed effects continue. (CR 5.1 and CR 7.)

## 10.5 Cards underneath another card

A card beneath another card is not independently in play. It normally has no active abilities or characteristics except those needed by a rule or effect. Boost and Shift can place cards underneath another card for different purposes. (CR 5.1, CR 8.4, CR 8.10.)

When the top card of a stack moves zones, underlying cards move or are handled according to the current stack and zone-change rules. Rules 2.2.0 clarify conflicting ready/drying states and cards beneath a shifted top card. Do not treat underlying cards as separate characters in play. (CR 5.1 and CR 8.10.)

## 11. Card types

## 11.1 Characters

Characters have cost, inkability, classifications, Strength, Willpower, lore, and abilities as printed or currently corrected. Characters can normally quest, challenge, sing, use abilities, and move to locations when eligible. (CR 5.3.)

## 11.2 Actions

An action is played, its effect resolves, and it is put into its owner’s discard. An action does not remain in play. (CR 5.4 and CR 6.7.)

### Songs

A song is an action subtype. A player may pay its ink cost normally or use the singing special rule when eligible. Singer and Sing Together provide additional ways to pay. Singing is an alternate cost; it does not change the song into another card type. (CR 5.4.4, CR 8.11-8.12.)

To sing a normal song, exert one ready character whose cost is at least the song’s cost, subject to drying and other restrictions. The character’s printed cost is used unless Singer or another effect changes how it counts for singing.

## 11.3 Items

Items enter play and remain until an effect or rule moves them. They can have static, triggered, or activated abilities. Items are not characters and cannot quest, challenge, sing, or move unless an effect explicitly gives the relevant permission. (CR 5.5.)

## 11.4 Locations

Locations enter play and have move cost, willpower, and sometimes lore. During the controller’s Set step, they produce lore equal to their current lore value. Opponents may challenge them. Locations do not normally challenge or deal challenge damage. (CR 5.6, CR 3.2.2, CR 4.6.)

Characters at a location remain characters in play; being at a location does not merge them into the location or protect them unless an effect says so.

## 12. Abilities and effects

## 12.1 General effect resolution

Read instructions in order. Complete each instruction as much as possible before proceeding, unless later text defines a dependency or simultaneous event. Punctuation and paragraph structure can separate instructions. (CR 6.1-6.2.)

### Choices

A player makes choices at the time required by the play or resolution process. A player cannot choose an illegal object. “Up to” can permit choosing zero. An instruction without “up to” generally requires the stated number when possible. (CR 6.2 and CR 6.7.)

### “May” effects

“May” makes the described instruction optional, but if choosing to perform it requires paying a cost or making a legal choice, the player must satisfy that requirement. Rules 2.2.0 clarify optional effects linked to costs. (CR 6.2.)

### “If” and dependent effects

An “if” condition can govern whether an instruction occurs, whether a later instruction depends on an earlier result, or whether a secondary choice is available. Evaluate it at the timing specified by the current conditional-dependent-effect rules rather than applying one blanket interpretation. (CR 6.2; CR 2.2.0 updates.)

### “For each”

“For each” calculates a quantity from the specified objects or events at the point required by the effect. Re-evaluate only if the rules or text instruct the game to do so. (CR 6.2.)

### Durations

Common durations include:

- This turn
- Until end of turn
- Until the start of your next turn
- While a condition remains true
- For as long as the source remains in the required zone

The source leaving play does not necessarily end an already-created effect with a defined duration. (CR 6.2 and CR 6.5.)

## 12.2 Triggered abilities and the bag

A triggered ability begins with timing language such as “when,” “whenever,” “at,” or another defined trigger construction. Whenever its condition is met, the ability is added to the bag by the player whose card generated it. If several triggered abilities have their conditions met at the same time, they are added simultaneously. If a condition is met while another effect is resolving, the ability is added to the bag immediately but waits to resolve until the current effect is completely resolved. (CR 6.2 and CR 7.7.3.)

The bag is not a player-controlled stack and players do not pre-order all waiting abilities. Use the official resolution procedure:

1. Finish the current effect, turn action, game action, or required step before resolving an ability from the bag.
2. Perform the applicable game-state check.
3. The bag checks which players have triggered abilities waiting.
4. If the active player has any, that player chooses one of their triggered effects and fully resolves it.
5. After that effect and the following game-state check, the same player continues choosing and resolving their effects until they have none remaining.
6. Pass the bag to the next player in turn order and repeat the same process.
7. Newly triggered abilities are added when their conditions are met. An ability generated by the currently resolving player can be seen at the next bag check; an ability generated by another player waits until the bag reaches that player.
8. When no triggered effects remain, the game continues with the active player.

Once triggered, an ability can exist independently of its source unless it needs information that cannot be determined after the source changes zones. (CR 6.2 and CR 7.7.)

## 12.3 Activated abilities

An activated ability is intentionally used by its controller by paying the stated cost. It can normally be activated during the controller’s Main phase unless its text or rules permit another time. Exerting, banishing, or paying ink before the divider is part of the cost. (CR 6.3.)

## 12.4 Static abilities

A static ability continuously modifies the game while its conditions are true and its source is in the zone where it functions. Static effects do not trigger and are not added to the bag. (CR 6.4.)

## 12.5 Replacement effects

A replacement effect changes an event before it happens. It can replace all or part of the event, including where a card goes or how damage is dealt. An event that is replaced does not also happen in its original form. If several replacement effects could apply, use the current affected-player, controller, and active-player procedures. (CR 6.5.)

## 12.6 Playing cards and resolving actions/effects

CR 6.7 provides detailed play and resolution procedures. Important distinctions:

- Choosing a card to play does not itself mean the card has been played.
- Costs are paid before the card resolves or enters play as required.
- On-play triggers occur only after the card has been successfully played.
- A free play is still a play and can trigger “when you play” abilities.
- A card put directly into play is not played unless the effect says to play it.
- An action finishes resolving before it goes to the discard and before a Vanish trigger caused by that action resolves. (CR 6.7 and CR 8.14.)

## 12.7 Modifiers

Modifiers can change cost, Strength, Willpower, lore, damage, classifications, abilities, or other characteristics. Apply them in the current rules order, accounting for base-setting effects, additions/subtractions, and restrictions. (CR 6 and applicable characteristic rules.)

When a modifier expires, immediately re-evaluate game-state checks. A character can be banished when a willpower increase ends if marked damage is now lethal.

## 13. Zones

## 13.1 Deck

The deck is ordered and hidden. Effects can inspect, reveal, search, shuffle, place cards on top or bottom, or move cards to another zone. Preserve the instructed order. (CR 7.2.)

## 13.2 Hand

A player can normally inspect their own hand but not an opponent’s. Cards in hand are not in play. An effect that reveals or discards from hand follows the exact quantity and choice rules. (CR 7.3.)

## 13.3 Play

Characters, items, and locations normally exist in Play. The Play zone contains objects controlled by the appropriate players, but ownership and control can differ. (CR 7.4.)

## 13.4 Inkwell

The inkwell contains cards used as ink. Cards there are normally facedown and do not retain normal card characteristics. Effects can place cards there ready or exerted and can inspect or interact with them only as permitted. (CR 7.5.)

## 13.5 Discard

The discard is a public, faceup zone. Its order is not normally gameplay-relevant unless a rule or effect says otherwise. Cards in discard are not in play. (CR 7.6.)

## 13.6 Revealed and temporary handling

A revealed card remains in its original zone unless the effect moves it. Temporary arrangements made while resolving a search or top-card effect do not create a new zone unless the rules define one. (CR 7.)

## 13.7 Bag

The bag is the rules structure for waiting triggered abilities. It is not a physical card zone and does not contain actions, characters, or cards. (CR 7.7.)

## 16. Rules-answer protocol

For every disputed interaction:

1. Confirm the format, event date, and rules version.
2. Confirm exact current English card identities and functional text.
3. Identify the proposed game event.
4. Identify all costs, permissions, requirements, and limiters.
5. Apply prohibitions before permissions.
6. Identify static and replacement effects before the event occurs.
7. Perform the event or play process in official order.
8. Add triggered abilities to the bag when their conditions are met; if a condition is met during another effect, the ability waits there until that effect finishes.
9. Resolve the current instruction completely.
10. Perform the applicable game-state check.
11. Resolve the bag using the active-player and pass-the-bag procedure, repeating checks until the bag is empty.
12. Cite the exact official section and disclose uncertainty.

Do not answer a tournament-impacting rules question solely from memory, card-art reminder text, an older printing, or a strategy guide.
