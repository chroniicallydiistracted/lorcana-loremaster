# Disney Lorcana Tournament Rules and Primary Formats

**Workspace review:** 2026-10-04. Retained policy digest updated to the current PDF; exact event instructions control where explicitly authorized.

**Project version:** 3.0
**Policy baseline:** Disney Lorcana Tournament Rules effective 2026-07-14
**Official Resources posting/update:** 2026-07-23
**Regional baseline:** United States / North America
**Formats covered:** Core, Infinity, Sealed, Draft, Preconstructed and Pack Rush

This is a retrieval-oriented tournament-policy digest. It does not replace the official Tournament Rules, the Comprehensive Rules, Play Correction Guidelines, or event-specific terms. An organizer may publish additional requirements for a particular event; those terms control when they are consistent with current official policy.

## 2. Tournament levels

Official events can be run at Entry, Competitive, or Premier level. Expectations become stricter as the level increases. Players at Competitive and Premier events are expected to know current rules, policy, deck registration requirements, and the Play Correction Guidelines. (TR 1.2.)

The same deck can be legal at several tournament levels while match structure, decklist requirements, enforcement, timing, and correction procedures differ. Always identify the event level before making a procedural claim.

# 3. Core Constructed

## 3.1 Deck construction

A Core Constructed deck must satisfy all of the following unless a legal card explicitly modifies deck construction: (TR 1.3.1.1.)

- At least 60 cards.
- No maximum size, provided the player can shuffle the deck promptly without assistance.
- No more than two ink types.
- Dual-ink cards are permitted only when all ink types on every card remain within the deck’s two-ink identity.
- No more than four copies of a card identified by its full English card name.
- Only cards legal in the current Core rotation.
- No cards on the current Core banned list.

For characters and locations, the full name includes the name and version. Two cards with the same character name but different versions have different full names and separate four-copy limits. Dual-named cards must be evaluated under current official naming and deck-construction rules, not by informal abbreviations.

## 3.2 Date-aware Project card pool

At the verified October 4, 2026 snapshot, Core uses Sets 9–13. Hyperia City (Set 14)
is published in the API and becomes eligible at its October 16 prerelease under
TR 1.6. Lists using new Set 14 identities before then are theorycrafting.

With Set 13, Sets 5–8 rotated; only Set 9 and later remain Core eligible, subject to
release dates, bans and event terms. Never use an API rotation flag alone to certify
legality. See `knowledge/formats/legality.md` for the policy snapshot and reprint logic.

## 3.3 Reprints and older physical printings

If a card is present in a set that is currently legal in Core, **any official printing of that same card** can be used in Core, including an older printing from a rotated set. (TR 1.6.)

Example: when `Stitch - Rock Star` is reprinted in a legal set, an older official printing of that same full card identity may be used.

Apply these controls:

- Confirm that the cards are the same official card identity, not merely characters with the same name.
- Apply current English functional text, classifications, and corrections if older printing text differs.
- Alternate, promo, special, and preview cards receive release and rotation status from their assigned set; they do not independently keep a card legal. (TR 1.6.)
- Use the current full English name for copy-limit verification.

## 3.4 Format-specific banned cards

The July 14, 2026 official policy lists **no current Core banned cards** and
**Hiram Flaversham – Toymaker banned in Infinity**. (TR 1.6.2.) The former project's
Hiram/Fortisphere Core-ban entries are superseded historical policy. Check the live
official documents and event terms before competitive registration.

## 3.5 Deck-construction modifiers

A legal card can modify ordinary deck construction. Apply only the permission stated by that card and verify its current official text. A modifier does not automatically waive minimum deck size, legality, bans, or requirements it does not mention. (Comprehensive Rules 1.10; TR 1.3.1.1.)

---

## 3.6 Infinity Constructed

Infinity follows ordinary Constructed size, ink and copy rules while permitting
cards from earlier sets and using its own banned list. Core rotation does not apply.
Release dates and special/encounter-card restrictions still matter. (TR 1.3.1.2.)

# 4. Sealed

## 4.1 General sanctioned Sealed rules

In Sealed, each player receives unopened booster packs and builds from that personal pool. (TR 1.3.2 and 1.3.2.2.)

Baseline requirements:

- At least six booster packs per player.
- Every player receives the same number of packs from each organizer-selected set.
- Deck minimum: 40 cards.
- No maximum other than the available pool.
- Any number of ink types.
- Any number of copies opened in the pool.
- The deck can contain only cards from that player’s supplied pool, subject to event terms.

Sealed can be run at any tournament level.

## 4.2 Product-specific six-pack example

A six-pack Attack of the Vine Sealed example uses:

- Six Attack of the Vine booster packs.
- In the standard Attack of the Vine prerelease configuration, each of the six booster packs contains 12 game cards, producing 72 game cards before deck selection. This is product- and event-specific, not a universal requirement for every sanctioned Sealed event.
- Build a deck of at least 40 cards from those cards.
- Use any number of inks.
- Use every copy opened if desired.
- Do not add cards from another set, collection, trade, or another player’s pool.

This is an example, not the Project’s current default. The official general Sealed rule permits organizer-selected set combinations; verify actual product composition, permitted extras and published event instructions. (TR 1.3.2.2.)

## 4.3 Pool registration and deck construction

When pool registration is required:

1. Open product only when instructed.
2. Confirm pack count and obvious product irregularities with a Lore Guide.
3. Register the entire pool accurately.
4. Build within the announced construction period.
5. Register the played deck if required.
6. Keep all pool cards together and identifiable throughout the event.

The recommended policy timing baseline includes 20 minutes for Sealed pool registration and 30 minutes for Sealed deck construction, although event terms can adjust timing. (TR 6.2.)

Do not consult notes during Limited deck construction unless event rules explicitly permit it. (TR 5.2.)

# 5. Draft

## 5.1 General Draft rules

In Draft, players select cards from booster packs in a pod, then build from the drafted pool. (TR 1.3.2 and 1.3.2.4.)

Baseline requirements:

- At least four booster packs per player.
- Every player begins with the same number of packs from each selected set.
- Deck minimum: 35 cards.
- Any number of ink types.
- Any number of copies drafted.
- Only cards drafted by that player can be used, subject to event terms.

## 5.2 Pods

Players are seated randomly into pods. Organizers should usually aim for pods close to eight players, normally at least six, while following the official pod guidance for the event’s size. (TR 1.3.2.4.)

At Entry-level Draft, pairings may cross pods. At Competitive Draft, players must be paired within their own pod. Organizers can announce special construction rules for a Competitive event in advance. (TR 1.3.2.4.)

## 5.3 Draft procedure

For the first pack:

1. Open the pack when instructed.
2. Select one card for the personal pool.
3. Pass the remaining cards left.
4. Receive the next packet from the right.
5. Continue until the pack is exhausted.

Passing reverses each pack: first left, second right, third left, and so on. Each pack travels in one direction only. Players should draft at a consistent pace and must not mix packets, pass the wrong direction, reveal hidden picks, or alter card counts. (TR 1.3.2.4.)

After all packs are drafted, the selected cards form the player’s pool. The policy baseline provides 25 minutes for Draft construction and registration together, with separate pick-time guidance available for higher-level events. (TR 6.2.)

## 5.4 Actual Draft environment

Use the actual event product/set(s). Do not apply six-pack or 40-card Sealed assumptions to Draft. Draft uses the event’s announced number of packs, with four as the general official minimum, and a 35-card minimum deck. (TR 1.3.2.4.)

# 6. Set legality at prerelease

A newly released set becomes legal for sanctioned Constructed and Limited play immediately at its hobby-store prerelease. (TR 1.6.)

For the North American Attack of the Vine release:

- Prerelease: 2026-07-17.
- Wide release: 2026-07-24.

The sanctioned legality point is the prerelease, not necessarily the wide-release date. Event terms can restrict the product used in a particular Limited event, but they cannot make an otherwise banned card legal without official authority.

# 7. Match structure

## 7.1 Entry and Competitive events

Entry and Competitive events may use different match structures chosen by the organizer. Set Championships and other named programs can impose additional official requirements. Events with 64 or more players are encouraged to use the Premier structure. (TR 3.3.)

Do not assume every local tournament is best-of-three or uses the same round length.

## 7.2 Premier structure

Premier Play uses best-of-three matches. The first player to win two games wins the match. Match points are normally: (TR 3.3.)

- 3 for a match win
- 1 for a match draw
- 0 for a match loss

Before game one, randomly determine which player chooses play or draw. That decision must be made before either opening hand is seen. The player who plays first skips the Draw step of their first turn. After a completed game, the loser of the most recent game chooses play or draw for the next game. (TR 3.3.)

In a single-elimination top cut, the higher seed normally chooses play or draw for game one, then the previous game’s loser chooses for the next game. Check event terms. (TR 3.3.)

# 8. Pregame and mulligan

Before each game: (TR 3.4; CR 2.2.)

1. Determine play/draw according to the match structure.
2. Each player sufficiently randomizes their deck.
3. Present the deck to the opponent, who may shuffle further or cut.
4. Draw seven cards.
5. Each player receives one mulligan opportunity.
6. Selected cards go to the bottom of the deck.
7. Draw replacements until the hand again contains seven cards.
8. Players who altered their hand shuffle after replacements are drawn.
9. The first player begins and skips the first-turn draw.

Pile counting is permitted only under the policy’s restrictions: before shuffling, once before each game, and never as a method of randomization or manipulation. (TR 3.4.)

# 9. Deck registration, deck checks, and pool integrity

When deck registration is required, the registered list must accurately match the deck and, for Limited, any required pool record. (TR 3.8.)

Players are responsible for:

- Correct quantities and full names.
- Core legality, ink identity, and copy limits.
- Accurate Sealed or Draft pool provenance.
- Maintaining the registered configuration where required.
- Presenting the deck promptly for checks.
- Replacing damaged sleeves or marked components.

A decklist label, archetype name, or online export does not override the physically registered deck. Call a Lore Guide when a discrepancy is discovered.

---


## 9.1 Limited registration and between-game deck use

The current general Tournament Rules do not establish one universal rule that either always permits or always prohibits rebuilding a Limited deck from unused pool cards between games or matches. TR 3.8 establishes deck-registration and deck-check requirements, including that the registered deck list is the ultimate arbiter when the physical deck and list differ, but that section alone does not define a general Limited sideboard policy.

Between-game or between-match Limited deck modification is controlled by the event’s terms, registration method, organizer instructions, and Lead Lore Guide. Preserve pool integrity, follow the announced registration procedure, and do not assume a universal permission or prohibition. (TR 1.3.2 and TR 3.8.)

# 10. Communication and in-game information

## 10.1 Public and private information

Current tournament policy uses two categories: **public information** and **private information**. The older “free/derived/hidden” taxonomy is not the current Lorcana policy. (TR 5.4.)

Private information includes information in facedown zones and the contents of a hand. Opponents are normally entitled to know only the number of cards in a private zone. Information in other zones is public. Information revealed from a private zone is public while it remains revealed. (TR 5.4.)

Players must communicate public game information accurately and maintain a clear game state.

## 10.2 Lore and card status

Players must announce lore changes and the new total. On request, a player must accurately communicate public status such as ready/exerted state, damage, drying status, and visible characteristics. Mandatory effects must be maintained and acknowledged; reminding an opponent of an optional “may” effect is encouraged but not mandatory. (TR 5.4.)

## 10.3 Standard tournament inking procedure

When taking the normal ink action: (TR 5.4-5.5.)

1. Announce the card’s full name.
2. Give the opponent an opportunity to verify its inkwell symbol.
3. Put it into the inkwell faceup for that turn.
4. Keep it clearly distinct from other facedown cards and consistently oriented as ready or exerted.
5. At the end of the turn, turn that card and any other faceup inkwell cards facedown.

This tournament verification procedure differs from a simplified casual description that immediately puts ink facedown.

# 11. Game layout and accessories

Cards in play must be arranged clearly, with characters, items, and locations distinct from the inkwell and other facedown areas. Ready and exerted orientation must be consistent. (TR 5.1 and 5.5.)

Use approved, non-marked sleeves and accessories. Accessories must not obscure public information, create confusion, provide outside information, or damage cards. Tournament officials can require replacement or removal. (TR 3.11 and 5.1.)

---

# 12. Notes, outside assistance, and electronic devices

Players cannot receive outside strategic assistance during a match or Limited construction. Notes are restricted, and players cannot consult notes while drafting or building a Limited deck. Writing down hidden inkwell identities or receiving information from another person can constitute outside assistance. (TR 5.2.)

Electronic devices such as smartphones, tablets, and smart watches are permitted at Disney Lorcana events when they remain in plain view of both players. Using the Disney Lorcana TCG Companion app to track lore and referring to the card database are expressly permitted examples. For private use such as texting, calling, or browsing, first inform a Lore Guide, who will facilitate the request. Hidden or noncompliant device use can result in corrective action. (TR 5.3.)

---

# 13. Pace of play, time, and end-of-round

Players must maintain a reasonable pace regardless of match score or board complexity. Deliberately using time to gain advantage is not permitted. Call a Lore Guide when pace becomes a concern rather than escalating with the opponent. (Tournament policy and Play Correction Guidelines.)

When time is called in a normal Swiss round: (TR 3.4.)

1. The active player finishes the current turn; this is turn 0.
2. Five additional turns are played.
3. If the game ends, report normally.
4. If it remains unfinished, that game is recorded as a draw and the match result follows the number of completed game wins.

Single-elimination end-of-round procedure can continue with additional tiebreak rules, including lore comparison after the specified turns. Always use the event’s current procedure. (TR 3.4.)

# 14. Out-of-order sequencing and takebacks

## 14.1 Out-of-order sequencing

Players can sometimes perform a technically reordered sequence when the final result is clear, no hidden information is gained, no opponent decision is bypassed, and the sequence can be understood as a legal intended line. It cannot be used to conceal choices, retroactively change a line after gaining information, or ignore a trigger window. (TR 3.5.)

## 14.2 Takebacks

A takeback is not an entitlement. Officials evaluate whether the request is immediate, whether new information was gained, whether choices or reactions occurred, and whether reversing the action preserves a fair game state. (TR 3.7.)

An illegal action or rules error should be stopped and referred to a Lore Guide. Players must not privately invent a repair at a sanctioned event. (TR 3.7.2; Play Correction Guidelines.)

---


## 14.3 Concessions and intentional draws

A player may concede a game or match, but a player cannot ask an opponent to concede. Players may agree to an intentional draw before the match ends; under the current Tournament Rules, the recorded result is 0-0-3. A player may request an intentional draw only once during a match, and no reward, consideration, or outside incentive may be offered or accepted in exchange for a concession or intentional draw. (TR 3.6.)

If players continue playing after an intentional-draw request is declined, they must continue normally without repeated pressure. Call a Lore Guide if a proposed concession or draw appears connected to bribery, wagering, collusion, or another prohibited incentive.

# 15. Corrections and judge calls

When a material error, hidden-information issue, deck problem, missed mandatory effect, or disagreement occurs:

1. Pause the game.
2. Preserve the current state.
3. Call a Lore Guide promptly.
4. Explain what happened in chronological order.
5. Do not move cards, recreate draws, or negotiate an unofficial rewind.
6. Follow the official ruling and remedy.

The [Play Correction Guidelines](https://files.disneylorcana.com/Disney_Lorcana_Play_Correction_Guidelines_052124update.pdf) guide warnings, rewinds, partial fixes, leave-as-is decisions, game or match losses, and disqualification. Intent and concealment can change the severity of a ruling.

---

# 16. Player responsibilities checklist

Before an event:

- Confirm event level, format, date, and region.
- Confirm current rules and Tournament Rules versions.
- Confirm Core rotation, reprint status, and bans.
- Confirm Sealed or Draft product and construction rules.
- Validate deck size, ink identities, full-name copy limits, and deck modifiers.
- Compare the registered list with the physical deck.
- Use uniform sleeves and bring replacements.
- Bring clear lore and damage tracking.

During an event:

- Randomize sufficiently and present the deck.
- Decide play/draw before seeing the opening hand.
- Follow the official mulligan order.
- Announce public changes and lore totals.
- Keep newly inked cards faceup for the turn.
- Maintain a clear layout and pace.
- Call a Lore Guide promptly.
- Report results accurately.
