# Limited: Sealed and Draft Framework

**Project version:** 3.0  
**Environment:** Exact supplied event/pool; Set 14 prerelease preparation is theorycrafting before October 16, 2026  
**Formats:** Sealed and Draft, analyzed separately  
**Regional baseline:** United States / North America

Limited decks are built from a supplied pool, not a collection. Reliable curve, inkability, character density, board impact, and flexible card quality normally matter more than Constructed-level synergy.

---

# 1. Official format baselines

## 1.1 Sealed

General sanctioned Sealed: (TR 1.3.2 and 1.3.2.2.)

- At least six booster packs per player.
- A minimum 40-card deck.
- Any number of ink types.
- Any number of copies from the pool.
- Only cards in the player’s supplied pool.

### Six-pack example; not a universal product default

For a six-pack Set 13 example:

- Six Attack of the Vine booster packs only.
- Twelve playable cards per pack, for 72 playable cards.
- Select at least 40 cards for the deck.
- Use any number of inks.
- Use any number of copies opened.
- Do not add cards from another set, personal collection, trade, or another player’s pool.

Event instructions control if an organizer announces a sanctioned variation.

## 1.2 Draft

General sanctioned Draft: (TR 1.3.2 and 1.3.2.4.)

- At least four booster packs per player.
- A minimum 35-card deck.
- Any number of ink types.
- Any number of copies drafted.
- Draft in pods, normally close to eight players and usually at least six.
- Pack one passes left, pack two right, then alternates.

Use the actual draft product. If omitted, state the assumed set and confirm before making set-specific picks.

## 1.3 Do not merge the formats

Sealed and Draft share pool-based construction but reward different decisions:

- **Sealed:** Evaluate the complete fixed pool and build the strongest available deck.
- **Draft:** Evaluate each pick’s power, flexibility, signals, commitment, curve, and opportunity cost.

A Sealed grade is not automatically a Draft pick order.

# 2. Modified B.R.E.A.D.

Use B.R.E.A.D. as a prioritization framework, not an automatic ranking formula.

## Bombs

A bomb can dominate a normal Limited game or create a major immediate advantage.

Ask:

- Does it stabilize from behind?
- Does it generate value immediately?
- Can it win without another rare or exact enabler?
- Is the cost realistic for the format?
- How broadly can opponents answer it?
- Does it remain useful when drawn late?

## Removal

Separate removal by function:

- Direct damage
- Banish
- Bounce
- Exert control
- Ready-character access
- Challenge modifier
- Item removal
- Location removal
- Area damage
- Lore interaction

Grade flexibility and reliability. Conditional removal drops when its setup is uncommon.

## Evasive

Evasive threats create a clock normal challengers cannot answer. Count:

- Evasive characters.
- Alert characters.
- Direct removal that can hit Evasive.
- Global or non-choosing interaction.
- Race tools.

## Approach / proactive curve

The deck must affect the board. Value:

- Early characters.
- Efficient Strength/Willpower bodies.
- Reliable lore.
- Favorable challenge breakpoints.
- Cards that are useful without synergy.

## Draw

Distinguish true card advantage from cycling, selection, tutoring, and conditional draw. Expensive draw can be poor when it sacrifices the board.

## Adjustments beyond B.R.E.A.D.

Always adjust for:

- Inkability.
- Cost curve.
- Character count.
- Ink depth.
- Floor and ceiling.
- Shift target/payoff density.
- Singer/song balance.
- Classification density.
- Item and location relevance.
- Synergy dependency.
- Number of narrow or dead cards.

# 3. Card grading system

Grade separately for Sealed and Draft.

- **S:** Game-dominating and highly reliable bomb or premium answer.
- **A:** Strong card that normally pulls the deck or pick toward its ink.
- **B:** Solid main-deck playable.
- **C:** Curve filler, role-player, or conditional playable.
- **D:** Narrow, low-floor, or pool-dependent.
- **F:** Rarely belongs in a normal main deck.

Record for every card:

- Sealed grade.
- Draft grade.
- B.R.E.A.D. category.
- Inkability.
- Cost and actual curve role.
- Character body.
- Lore pressure.
- Floor and ceiling.
- Immediate board impact.
- Synergy requirement.
- Removal vulnerability.
- Best targets or partners.
- Cards that make the grade rise or fall.

# 4. Sealed pool audit

Before building:

1. Verify every card name and quantity against the catalog.
2. Confirm actual pack/product configuration, set(s), permitted extras and total personal pool size.
3. Separate cards by ink, cost, type, and inkability.
4. Mark bombs, removal, Evasive, Alert, draw, Shift, singers, songs, items, and locations.
5. Count playable characters in each ink.
6. Identify standalone cards versus synergy-dependent cards.
7. Note dual-ink cards and every ink identity they add to a candidate deck.

Do not treat foil and non-foil copies as different gameplay cards.

# 5. Sealed candidate-deck process

## 5.1 Evaluate ink combinations

Sealed permits any number of inks. That does not mean every strong card should be included.

For each candidate combination, calculate:

- Total playables.
- Character count.
- Inkable/non-inkable count.
- Turns 1-4 board plays.
- Bombs.
- Removal.
- Evasive threats and answers.
- Draw and selection.
- Shift targets and payoffs.
- Songs and singers.
- Item/location package.
- Weak cards required to reach 40.
- Ink/classification synergy requirements and the opportunity cost of omitting stronger off-theme cards. Lorcana ink is not color-specific payment.

Prefer the strongest complete 40+ card deck rather than the pile containing the most high-rarity cards.

## 5.2 Curve

Count cards that actually affect the board at each turn, not merely cards with that printed cost.

Prioritize:

- Reliable 2-cost characters.
- Enough 3-cost development.
- 4-cost stabilizers.
- A limited number of expensive finishers.
- Inkable cards across the curve.

A reactive 2-cost action does not replace a turn-two character when the deck needs board presence.

## 5.3 Character density

Characters normally supply questing, challenges, singing, Shift bases, classifications, and location movement. Avoid a deck overloaded with actions, items, or locations unless the pool supplies a complete and reliable engine.

## 5.4 Synergy threshold

Include a synergy package when:

- Enabler and payoff counts are sufficient.
- The cards are acceptable independently.
- The curve can assemble them.
- The payoff changes normal games.
- The deck does not require one rare to function.

Reject packages that require weak filler or unrealistic combinations.

## 5.5 Inkability

Do not use a universal non-inkable limit. Evaluate:

- How many non-inkables are expensive.
- How many must be held rather than inked.
- Whether the deck has draw/selection.
- Whether opening hands can ink through early turns.
- Whether splash cards have sufficient support; Lorcana ink is not color-specific payment.

# 6. Draft pick system

For each pick, weigh:

- Raw power.
- Inkability.
- Flexibility.
- Current curve.
- Character count.
- Removal need.
- Existing synergy.
- Ink commitment.
- Signals.
- Replaceability.
- Expected chance to see a similar card later.
- Opportunity cost of passing alternatives.

## Early picks

Prefer flexible bombs, premium removal, strong inkable characters, and cards with low synergy dependence. Do not lock into an ink because of one early rare.

## Middle picks

Use repeated card flow to identify open inks. Fill missing curve points and determine whether synergy density is achievable. A pivot is correct when incoming quality exceeds the sunk cost of earlier picks.

## Late picks

Complete the curve, character count, inkability, and alternate pool options; substitutions require event permission. Take narrow synergy only when the deck already supports it. Hate-draft only when no card meaningfully improves the deck and the denied card is unusually impactful.

## Signals

One late card is not proof that an ink is open. Use repeated strong cards, late premium commons/uncommons, and several packs of consistent information. Pack collation and neighboring drafters create noise.

# 8. Limited game plan

## Mulligan

Keep hands that can ink and affect the board. Return expensive bombs when the hand cannot survive to cast them. Keep narrow interaction only when the opposing deck or prior game makes it important.

## Quest versus challenge

Quest when the race is favorable and the opponent cannot profitably punish exertion. Challenge when the trade prevents more future lore, removes an engine, protects several future quests, or is required to stabilize.

## Removal discipline

Save premium answers for:

- Bombs.
- Repeatable engines.
- Evasive clocks.
- Threats that change the race immediately.
- Cards that protect a larger board.

Use cheaper interaction on ordinary threats when delaying will cost more lore than saving the answer gains.

## Playing from ahead

Protect the lore clock, avoid unnecessary trades, and do not overextend into known area effects.

## Playing from behind

Identify the minimum stabilization line. Take calculated risks when normal value play cannot catch up. A powerful card that does not affect the immediate race may be too slow.

# 9. Final Sealed output standard

A complete pool answer must provide:

- Verified actual pool size, product configuration and quantities.
- Separate card grades.
- Candidate ink combinations.
- Exact final 40+ card deck.
- Excluded playables and why.
- Inkable/non-inkable count.
- Cost and actual-play curve.
- Card-type and character counts.
- Bombs, removal, Evasive threats, and answers.
- Shift/song/classification packages.
- Mulligan priorities.
- Inking priorities.
- Early, middle, and late game plan.
- Pressure and recovery plan.
- Matchup substitutions if pool changes between games are allowed by event terms.
- Confidence and unverified card interactions.

# 10. Final Draft-pick output standard

A pick answer must provide:

- Recommended card.
- Next-best alternatives.
- Current deck needs.
- Signal interpretation.
- Commitment and flexibility impact.
- Curve and character-count impact.
- Risk.
- Future pick priorities.
